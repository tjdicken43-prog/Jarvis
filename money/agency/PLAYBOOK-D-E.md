# Playbook: ads, lead pages and Quote Assistant (D and E)

Written 2026-10-25 for TJ: what a professional local-marketing shop does that we would otherwise miss, and how to run it well. Prices are PROPOSED (see ADDONS.md). Market figures come from agency blogs found by web search on 2026-10-25, so they are directional, not audited. Nothing here is built; build a piece only after a client says yes.

## 1. Biggest finding: sell Google Local Services Ads before Search ads
- Search ads in these trades are expensive. Agency blogs put plumbing and HVAC clicks at about $15 to $50 each. At a 10 to 15% click-to-call rate that is roughly $100 to $300 a lead. A $500 budget buys only a handful of calls a month, too thin to judge anyone's work.
- Local Services Ads (LSA, the "Google Guaranteed" box at the top) bill per lead, not per click. Blogs put plumbing and HVAC leads around $20 to $100, about $63 average in one September 2026 benchmark. A lead is an answered call or a voicemail, and Google credits invalid leads if disputed.
- LSA needs the shop's state license, insurance, and a background check; blogs say 3 to 4 weeks to verify. **Start that paperwork on day one**, in parallel with everything else, because it is the slowest step.
- So the ladder becomes: LSA first, Search second (only for services LSA does not cover, or after LSA proves the shop's calls turn into jobs), skip Performance Max. Minimum Search budget we accept: $1,000/mo for HVAC and plumbing. Roofing is seasonal (after storms): run bursts, not flat budgets.

## 2. Lead Boost: how a pro structures it
**Pricing (proposed, replaces the flat $400 to $600 guess):**
| Tier | Ad spend | Our fee |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | up to $750/mo (mostly LSA) | $350 flat |
| Standard | $750 to $2,000 | $500 flat |
| Growth | over $2,000 | 15% of spend |
Market: percentage-of-spend is 10 to 20%, retainers $1,500+ for agencies. We sit under both on purpose. Month-to-month after a 3-month start; 30 days to cancel; no promise of lead counts or rankings.

**Setup checklist (per client, in order):**
1. Signed authorization: we run ads inside the CLIENT's own Google Ads account via a manager (MCC) link. They own the account, billing card and data. We never run their ads from our account and never hold their card. If they leave, the account stays theirs.
2. Verified Google Business Profile, license number and insurance proof, LSA application submitted (step 1 of the wait).
3. Conversion tracking: Google call forwarding number plus call recording where allowed; GA4 on the site; form and click-to-call events. Without this we cannot show results, so we do not start ads.
4. Call recording notice: tell callers the call may be recorded (Front Desk greeting already says it is an automated assistant). Arkansas is one-party consent but callers come from other states, so disclose always.
5. Geo radius = the towns the shop really serves. Ad schedule matches Front Desk so after-hours calls are answered.
6. Print the shop's license number on ads, quotes and the site. An Arkansas State Police rule found 2026-10-25 requires name plus license number (or a website or toll-free number) on licensee advertising; I could not confirm it covers plumbing or HVAC, so we do it for every shop either way.
7. Ad copy: no "licensed", "insured", "best", "24/7" or price claims unless verified and true (Google ad policy, FTC). Drafted by Jarvis, approved by the owner.
8. Negative keyword list from day one (jobs, DIY, free, training, parts, wholesale).

**Weekly and monthly routine:**
- Weekly (10 to 20 min a client): search terms report, add negatives, dispute invalid LSA leads, check budget pacing, check calls got answered.
- Close the loop: every Friday Front Desk texts the owner one question, "Which of this week's calls became jobs, and about how much?" That is the number that proves value, and ad platforms cannot give it.
- Monthly one-page report: spend, calls, answered calls, booked jobs, cost per booked job, what we changed, what we do next. Same format every month.
- Rule from CLAUDE.md holds: Jarvis recommends, a human approves every budget or bid change. Ad spend is the client's money, never the business card.

**Why this wins against agencies:** they cannot see what happens after the click. We answer the phone (Front Desk), so we can report "ad calls answered after hours: N" and "missed calls recovered". Lead with that in the pitch.

## 3. Local lead pages: how to do it without getting penalized
- Only pages for a service plus town the shop truly serves, each with something unique (real jobs, photos, answers local to that town). No city-clone batches: Google's scaled-content spam policy. This is why true rank-and-rent stays skipped.
- Never create fake or virtual addresses to claim a town on Maps; Business Profile policy treats that as spam and can suspend the listing.
- Include: LocalBusiness schema, consistent name, address, phone everywhere, service-area setup, review links, photos, a tracked phone number (static number shown on the page, the main number in schema, so listings stay consistent).
- Expect SEO to take 3 to 6 months. Sell it as "foundation", not "leads next week". Price floor $99 only when bundled with Care; $199 stand-alone.

## 4. Quote Assistant (E): how a pro adds it
- Honest competition: Jobber, Housecall Pro and similar already do quoting for roughly $50 to $200+ a month. Our edge is not software. It is (a) the quote starts from the Front Desk call summary, (b) it drafts from the owner's rough notes or a voice memo in their own words and price book, (c) it chases the quote automatically. The follow-up is where the money is; most lost quotes die in silence.
- Concierge first: do the first three clients by hand with Claude (owner texts notes, Jarvis drafts, owner sends). Build nothing until three people pay. Pros pre-sell before they build.
- Intake (30 minutes per shop): their price book (hourly rate, markup, common jobs), standard terms, license number, logo, good/better/best options.
- Every quote carries: license number, scope, price, what is not included, validity date, payment terms. No invented prices, no promised timelines, owner approves and sends. Roofing and insurance claims: no statements about what insurance will cover.
- Follow-ups: day 2, day 5, day 10, owner-approved wording. TCPA: texts only to people who asked for the quote, from the shop's own number, with opt-out; no purchased lists. Email is the safe default. This keeps within the no-AI-cold-texting rule.
- Pricing: $49 drafting only; $79 with follow-ups. Measure quote-to-job rate before and after so we can show the lift.

## 5. Steps a pro does that we were missing
1. **Pre-sell before building.** Offer to the first 3 to 5 shops that reply; founding-client deal (first 5 lock their price) applies. Build only on yeses.
2. **Contract and authorization.** One page: scope, fee, month-to-month, client owns accounts, no guarantees, ad spend is client's, data handling. Lawyer glance already on the pro-moves list; add this page to it.
3. **Insurance.** Ask the E&O quote (already pending) whether it covers ad-management mistakes; many policies exclude overspend.
4. **Guardrails against our own mistakes:** daily budget caps set in Google, spend alert at 80% pacing, a pause switch, and a rule that we never raise a budget without the owner's reply in writing (text counts).
5. **Package ladder in the pitch:** Good = Front Desk $149, Better = Plus $249, Best = Growth (Plus + Quote Assistant + lead pages + review replies, bundle price proposed $449, Lead Boost priced separately because it depends on ad spend). Three choices close better than eight, so show the ladder; keep the full menu for after they say yes.
6. **Order of offers:** Front Desk first, reviews in month 1, Quote Assistant month 2, Lead Boost after 4+ weeks of call data, lead pages month 3.
7. **Numbers to track** (add to the weekly note once clients exist): attach rate (add-ons per client), churn, hours spent per client, gross margin per package, cost to win a client. Targets: 25% attach by month 3, under 3 hours a client a month.
8. **Capacity:** one person with templates handles about 15 to 20 ad accounts. Jarvis drafts and reports; a human clicks approve. Do not use the Google Ads API until it is worth the developer-token review.
9. **Taxes:** fees follow money/TAX.md like other income. Ad spend is paid by the client directly, so it is not our revenue.
10. **Exit rule:** if a package has fewer than 2 paying clients after 60 days, it is dropped from the menu (same shutdown rule as money/LANES.md).

## Price check (2026-10-25, web search; no Bentonville or NWA agency prices were published, so these are national benchmarks)
- Google Ads management for small local businesses: about $500 to $1,500 a month in less competitive markets, $1,000 to $2,500 as a common small-business range, and more in big metros ([ClicksGeek guides](https://clicksgeek.com/?p=22409), an agency blog). NWA is a smaller market, so the low end fits. Our Lead Boost tiers ($350 / $500 / 15%) sit under that band, which is a reason to start at $500 for Standard rather than discount further.
- Local SEO for home-service businesses: about $750 to $3,000 a month as agency retainers. Our lead pages at $99 to $199 are cheap by comparison; fine for a foundation, and a reason not to promise rankings.
- Review tools and receptionists, for the whole ladder: AI receptionists cost about $30 to $300 a month; Wakeman lists After Hours $149, Front Desk $349, Dispatch $829 ([vendor guide](https://www.open.cx/blog/ai-for-plumbers-2026)); human answering services start around $132 and run $550 to $1,500. Our $149 and $249 sit in the middle of the AI range. No change recommended.
- To get real NWA numbers: ask for a quote from two Bentonville or Fayetteville agencies by phone (needs a human voice, not a cold AI call), or ask the first shops who reply what they pay now.
