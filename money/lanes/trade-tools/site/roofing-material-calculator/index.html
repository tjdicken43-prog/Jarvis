How the math works

A roof is longer than the building under it. For a pitch of p inches of rise per 12 inches of run, the roof surface is the footprint times the square root of 1 + (p/12)². At 6/12 that is 1.118, at 12/12 it is 1.414.

One roofing square is 100 square feet. Waste covers cuts at hips, valleys and edges, plus the odd damaged bundle. The percentages here (10, 15, 20) are common rules of thumb. If your supplier uses a different number, trust theirs.

Starter and drip edge are figured from the eave and rake length. With a length and width we assume 2 × (length + width), which is right for a simple gable or hip roof with no overhang changes. Ridge cap uses the longer side as the ridge. On anything more complicated, measure the real runs from the ground or a drone shot and type your own numbers into a roof area calculation.

Worked example

A 40 by 30 foot house at 6/12, a hip and a valley. Footprint 1,200 sq ft × 1.118 = 1,342 sq ft, or 13.4 squares. Add 15% waste: 15.4 squares. At 3 bundles a square, that is 47 bundles. Underlayment: 2 rolls at 10 squares each.

Questions

How do I measure pitch?
Hold a level flat against the rafter or deck edge, 12 inches out, and measure straight up to the roof. That number is the rise. From a ladder, a phone level app gets you within a step.
How many bundles are in a square?
Most architectural and three-tab shingles ship 3 bundles to the square. Some heavier lines use 4. The wrapper says.
Does this include a tear-off?
No. It lists new material only. Tear-off, disposal, decking repair, flashing and vents change the price, so add them as lines in the estimate maker.
Is it accurate enough to order from?
It is accurate enough to price a job and to check a supplier's list. Order from a measured takeoff on complex roofs.
